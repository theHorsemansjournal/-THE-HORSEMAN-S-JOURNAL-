Who I Am

My name is Praveen Kumar. I am not a trainer in the traditional sense. I do not fix horses. I do not dominate them. I do not ask them to perform tricks or win ribbons. I work with them until they no longer feel the need to resist. That is the only definition of horsemanship that has ever made sense to me.

For over eleven years, I have lived inside the world of horses. Stallions, warmbloods, creatures of instinct and ancient grace who have no use for pretense. They did not simply teach me their language. They remade me, slowly, from the inside out, without asking permission and without offering explanations.

What sets me apart is not a certification or a trophy. It is the thousands of hours spent in silence with these animals, learning to read what they do not say. I have worked with horses that others gave up on. I have sat with fear until it softened. I have earned trust the only way it can be earned — one honest moment at a time.

The Journey

I came to horses ordinary. Quiet in a way that felt like smallness rather than strength. The world outside the stable never quite held a shape I was built for. But inside that world, surrounded by warmth and breath and the particular weight of a thousand-pound animal standing still beside you, something in me could finally exhale.

I did not begin with a philosophy. I began like most people do — trying to make things work, trying to control outcomes, trying to get the horse to respond correctly. Over time, that approach stopped making sense. The horses obeyed. Mostly. They performed. But when the work was done and the lead rope went slack, they never turned toward me. Something was always held back.

"A horse does not ask what you want. It asks what you are."

That realization took years. And it cost me. It cost me the comfort of certainty, the ease of old habits, the illusion that I was in control. But what it gave me in return was immeasurable — a way of being with horses that felt true, not just effective.

The Black Stallion

Then the black stallion arrived, and everything changed. He was four years old, barely schooled. A living storm wrapped in midnight muscle and something older than training. He was not stubborn. He was honest. And from the very first day, he read me with a precision that cut through every mask I had carefully constructed over years of training.

He taught me that strength was never the point. What a horse asks for has nothing to do with dominance or performance. It asks you to stand without fear, to be consistent without agenda, to become the kind of presence an intelligent creature of pure instinct chooses to trust. That is a much harder thing than it sounds.

"The horse that obeys because it has no choice is not truly trained. It is simply waiting for an opportunity to choose differently."

The Philosophy

A horse must be able to think before it can learn. If the horse is overwhelmed, no amount of pressure will create clarity.

Behaviour is information, not disobedience. Every reaction shows you something about the horse's internal state.

Willingness is more valuable than obedience. A correct response given freely is fundamentally different from one that is forced.

Emotional steadiness is not optional. It is the foundation of everything. A horse cannot find clarity through a handler who is internally conflicted or externally aggressive.

Trust is built in small moments. Not in grand gestures, but in the thousand small honest interactions that tell the horse you are safe, consistent, and real.

Why I Write

This journal is not a training manual. It is a conversation — about trust, about fear, about the invisible bond between horse and human. It is the record of eleven years of mornings in arenas, of falls and breakthroughs, of long, hard silences where nothing is said and everything is understood.

I write because the horses taught me something I could not keep to myself. They taught me that the thing I was hiding was the thing that was safe. The thing I was performing was the thing that drove them away. I had it backwards my entire life.

"The horse does not ask you to be perfect. It asks you to be real."

Every article, every essay, every word on this site has been lived first in an arena, in the dust, in the space between my breath and a horse's heartbeat. I do not write theory. I write what I have learned from animals that cannot speak — and that is exactly why they are the best teachers.

Beyond Horses

When I am not working with horses, I am reading, writing, or walking in silence. I believe that the same principles that build trust with a horse build trust with anything alive — patience, consistency, and the willingness to listen without an agenda.

I am based in India, where the equestrian world is still growing. My perspective is different because my journey is different. I did not grow up with horses. I found them. And that act of finding — of choosing this path against the grain — has shaped everything about my approach.

I am still afraid sometimes. Even now. Even after everything. Fear does not disappear because you have learned to work alongside it. But I stand. Not because I would lose something if I walked away. But because I refuse to abandon the one thing that has carried me through all of this — the work, the horses, and the truth they have taught me.
